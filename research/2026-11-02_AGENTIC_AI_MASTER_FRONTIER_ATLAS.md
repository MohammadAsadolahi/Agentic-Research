# Agentic AI emerging-field master atlas — coverage audit, not a novelty claim

**As of:** 2026-11-02. **Owner:** Agentic-Research. **Objective:** consolidate our repeated frontier scans into one broad, skeptical map, and test for major missing *families*, instead of endlessly promoting the latest paper title. **Status:** literature map only; no original agent experiments, method or acceptance-probability claim.

## Honest completeness standard

No one can know **all** new emerging fields in an actively changing global literature; terminology, disciplinary boundaries and unpublished work make that impossible. We can be confident about coverage of the **major discoverable field families up to this date**, not every niche/subfield, unpublished method or paper. The map currently spans **18 research families and 111 named problem areas**, including **mature** material expressly to prevent novelty-by-renaming. Those 111 categories are an **investigator-authored taxonomy** rather than objectively distinct fields, or a validated count of emerging fields. Duplicates and overlaps are intentional when questions cross disciplinary boundaries.

**Evidence method:** targeted arXiv primary search in October/September and earlier 2026 across AI/multiagent systems, information retrieval, networking/systems, cryptography/security, robotics, HCI/social sciences, public-sector/healthcare, experimental science, energy and digital twins. Spot-checked the original arXiv abstracts for key anchors and prior public-code artifacts; reviewed all existing project draft scans #3–#9. Search was **not** an exhaustive arXiv API export, bibliography snowball to saturation, formal meta-analysis or detailed method/code audit of every paper. In particular there are likely unobserved disciplinary and language-specific niches. Topic maturity and feasibility ratings are qualitative.

**Legend:** **E** = emerging *as an LLM/tool-using agent-specific inquiry* (not a novelty claim); **T** = transitional/agent-specific formulation of a mature topic; **M** = mature established umbrella research.

## Particularly important newly broadened areas from this last scout

The earlier reports heavily emphasized agent societies, continual learning, shared state, agentic web, autonomous science, delegation and infrastructure. These **additional fronts** prevent our search from being anchored to the same old favorites:

1. **Local/personal agent execution and data-exposure tradeoffs:** [AgBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38652) explicitly benchmarks device/cloud/hybrid autonomy; source reports that no single architecture dominates goodput, success, cost and exposure. New agent-centric policy question conceivable, but not proof of a novelty gap.
2. **Public-sector procedural validity:** [Agent Benchmarks Fail Public Sector Requirements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20617) analyzes 1,300+ benchmark papers and finds none simultaneously meets its process/realism/public-sector/metric criteria. Domain expertise and genuine operational evidence are major requirements. *This is a benchmark-design research direction, not automatically a new agent algorithm.*
3. **Agent risk accumulation and insurance:** [Insurance of Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05449) introduces an actuarial framework for autonomous action and correlated dependencies. Conceptual, and insurance is mature; independent operational loss data hard to get.
4. **Proof-backed autonomous actions:** [Cryptographic certificates of validity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23768) proposes predicate-to-cryptographic-certificate approach; [Verifiable Agentic Infrastructure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15228) already develops proof-derived authorization, with classic proof-carrying code and distributed consensus as strong precedents. Don't call action proofs new.
5. **Agentic live cyber-defense environments:** [CyberArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19005) has concurrent, dynamic attack–defense agent episodes; [CWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17020) assesses auditable repair under held-out vulnerabilities. Strong executable agent science but needs sandboxing, research ethics and direct benchmark methods.
6. **Agent skill-composition security:** [KnotScryer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32155) and [CoSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35082) cover interactions between individually safe skills, with benchmarks and guards. Generic 'check composed skill flows' is not available as first contribution.
7. **Agent–robot federated authority:** [FSAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11028) and [Embodied Collective Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27929) explicitly study collaboration across robot identities, authority and shared execution contexts. Distinguish from mature multi-robot coordination research; hardware/simulation realism burden is high.
8. **Agentic wireless/industrial digital twins:** [Agentic WDT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01437), [Industrial Autonomous Digital Twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20761), and a [547-paper MAS+digital-twin review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21873) preempt generic first-to-integrate claims. Domain-specific calibration may be scientifically interesting.
9. **Scientific benchmark self-evolution:** [AutoSciBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05140) already builds verified automatically evolving scientific tasks; combined evaluator and generator is not a first idea.
10. **Agentive social-science simulator validation:** [SLALOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11466) already studies plausibility of the *trajectory*, not just an endpoint; classical pattern-oriented modeling and simulator validation are foundational.
11. **Healthcare-specific stateful operation:** [MedHarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15714) tests hundreds of executable care workflows and harness properties; [HealthAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31179) provides additional real-style environments. Requires clinical expertise/ethical constraints; do not equate benchmark task success with patient safety.
12. **Public-health agentic evidence assembly:** [Public Health Data Collection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31746) demonstrates evidence collection/source discovery and warns prospective timeliness is not yet validated. The hard question is validity of evidence in changing surveillance environments.
13. **Security behavior after installing a specialized tool harness:** [Do AI-for-Science Agents Preserve Model Safety?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32045) reports base-model safeguards can fail when embedded in a powerful scientific agent. Strong crossover with established prompt-injection/security work; no unsafe experimentation outside sandbox.
14. **Defense-quality rather than attack-only agent evaluation:** [CWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17020) uses executable repair checks and functionality retention, while [CyberArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19005) tests dynamic symmetry; generic scanner and static CTF are insufficient novelty.
15. **Agentic Internet of Agents architecture:** [Internet of Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12835) and [Agent-OSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13795) already articulate full discovery, protocols, trust and payment layers; do not claim a first comprehensive stack.

## Strongest field-level method-finding prospects for a small team

This section is **not a list of proven gaps**. Scoring is a judgment of: agentic specificity, importance, measurable objective, baseline availability, and ability to avoid expensive proprietary infrastructure.

1. **Decentralized agentic-web interaction under independently owned provider policies** — executable AgentWebBench public code; prior art in federated search, provider incentives and source diversity is formidable. Candidate outcome: end-to-end task correctness and provider burden at equal cost. **Conditional A**.
2. **Agent-mediated scientific evidence validity through stateful instruments** — high scientific signal, q-devBench/instrMCP public work; original benchmark repo lookup previously returned 404, so full reproducibility unconfirmed. Classical metrology and Bayesian experimental design are hard baselines. **Conditional A**.
3. **Autonomous capability investment/bottling under workload shifts** — excellent code/metric baseline BOTTLED, with strong fixed distillation comparators. Active learning/AutoML/optimal stopping may dominate; GPU/API cost. **Conditional A**.
4. **Cross-principal evidence acquisition in real A2A workflows** — genuine private-state tool task question; verification, signing and value-of-information classical methods strong. **Conditional A**.
5. **Adaptive on-device/hybrid agent offloading with data-exposure constraints** — AgBench benchmark; classical constrained scheduling and split-compute already exist. Possible high-practicality systems method with actual device testing. **Conditional B+**.
6. **Operational evaluation comparability and evidence freshness for evolving agents** — real rater-effects study; statistics/sequential decisions sophisticated. Potentially more a measurement paper than a new agentic algorithm. **Conditional B+**.
7. **Public-sector procedure-aware autonomous task evaluation and authorization** — benchmark paper reports deficiency, actual records require special domain knowledge; generic process-constraint checks already old. **Conditional B**.
8. **Agent-population ecological stability, capacity and churn** — foundational difficulty, nontrivial classical ecology/queueing baselines, needs many real-agent runs; very high compute/method burden. **Conditional B**.
9. **Agent-human persistent organizational action** — DotSpace a paradigm paper; needing real people/independent user principals is a constraint. **Conditional B**.
10. **Scientific simulator-to-reality validity** — important, difficult without real deployment observations, classic causal/transport methods; **conditional B**.
11. **Certified action authorization** — strong formal-method depth but direct proofs/auth proposals already and significant cryptographic expertise; **conditional B** for security/systems.
12. **Collective evidence and social-science simulator validity** — SLALOM and other methods already; independent observational data needed; **conditional B**.

**Avoid as default** for our project: generic memory/planning/reflection, generic agent transaction wrappers, first attention inbox, first reversible execution, generic scientific critic, first reputation system, generic skill-composition checking, generic multi-agent GPU scheduling, first agent benchmark generator, first social-agent simulation. Most are mature or direct 2026 methods.

## What would justify saying "we have adequately covered the frontier"?

**Coverage criterion reached at coarse-grained level:** scans have now traversed at least 18 umbrella families covering individual agents, multi-agent societies, networking/markets, web, security/safety, governance, measurement, software, infrastructure, scientific/physical autonomy, sectoral applications and sustainability. Recent cross-sector searches exposed new subfields *inside* existing families (e.g., digital twins, devices, public administration, proof-backed action), not a previously unrecognized entire agentic-AI macrofamily. This is an **investigator's saturation judgment, not a measured saturation curve**.

**Still not comprehensive in the strict sense:** arXiv category searches miss discipline-specific journals, workshops, industry work, patents, multilingual research, and papers that do not use "agent" terms. No source can certify coverage of unreleased publications. Claiming literally **all** emerging fields would be false. We should stop *generic scouting rounds* because they are now mostly relabeling neighbors and spend effort on an evidence-based falsification experiment.

**Minimum next work:** conduct a direct methodological/code audit of the two best available candidate platforms (AgentWebBench and BOTTLED/AgBench, depending on resources) and reproduce real agent-run baselines. Or choose a domain-specialist collaborator for instrument metrology. Do not propose a method until a strong-baseline residual is measured.

## Research state and corrections

- **No original LLM agent experiment** was run in this round.
- **No novel research gap** was established, and no acceptance probability can be inferred.
- Paper titles, abstracts and reported numbers are from sources, not independent reproducibility.
- Classification counts are analyst-written descriptors, not a scientific bibliometric result.
- This atlas supersedes serial "best field" pivots from earlier reports as a **stable coverage map**.
